````
# Shelfwise interview guide v1

## Intro script (read aloud)
"Thanks for talking with me. I'd like to hear how deliveries get received and recorded in your shop, and there are no right or wrong answers, because I'm learning about the process, not testing you. Is it okay if I take notes or record this, for the research team only?"

Scope: receiving a delivery and recording it in stock (see brief.md).
Ask about the most recent real delivery where you can. Avoid yes/no and leading follow-ups.

## Research question 1: In which step do mistakes or delays happen when receiving a delivery?
1. Walk me through the last delivery you received, from the moment it arrived until the stock was updated.
2. Tell me about a time a delivery took longer to get through than you expected. What was happening?
3. Which part of receiving a delivery do you find yourself checking twice, and why?

## Research question 2: Where do differences come from, and how are they fixed once found?
1. Tell me about the last time what arrived didn't match what you ordered. What did you do?
2. When you find a difference, who else gets involved, and how do you sort it out with them?
3. Describe a difference you only noticed later, after the delivery was already recorded. How did it come to light?

## Research question 3: What limits does counting at the shelf put on recording in the app?
1. Describe how you keep track of your counts between the shelf and the computer.
2. Think of the last time you counted a delivery. What happened between finishing the count and entering it in the app?
3. What do you have with you when you count at the shelf, and what do you wish you had?

## Research question 4: Why is recorded stock different from real stock, and how much do users trust it?
1. Tell me about the last time the number in the app didn't match what was on the shelf. What did you think had happened?
2. When you need to know how much of something you have, how do you find out?
3. In what situations do you rely on the app's stock numbers, and in what situations do you check another way?
````

````
# Interview Guide v1

Shelfwise: receiving a delivery and recording it in stock.
For shop owners and stock keepers. These are open questions. Ask about real, recent deliveries, not about what usually happens.

## Intro script

"Thanks for making time for this. I'd like to learn how deliveries are handled in your shop, from when they arrive to when they're in stock. There are no right or wrong answers, and I'm not testing you or the app, so please tell me how things really happen, even if it's not how they're meant to."

## Research question 1: In which step do mistakes or delays happen when receiving a delivery?

1. Tell me about the last delivery you received, from the moment it arrived until you finished with it.
2. Which part of handling a delivery takes you the longest? Why do you think that is?
3. Think of a delivery that didn't go smoothly. What happened?

## Research question 2: Where do differences come from, and how are they fixed once found?

1. Tell me about the last time what arrived didn't match what you ordered. How did you notice?
2. What did you do about it after that, in the shop, in the app, and with the supplier?
3. When the goods and the paperwork don't match, what do you think usually causes it?

## Research question 3: What limits does counting at the shelf put on recording in the app?

1. Walk me through how you count a delivery. Where are you standing, and what do you have with you?
2. What happens after you've finished counting?
3. Tell me about a time something got lost or changed between counting and recording.

## Research question 4: Why is recorded stock different from real stock, and how much do users trust it?

1. Tell me about the last time the number in the app didn't match what was on the shelf. What did you do?
2. When you look at a stock number in the app, how do you decide whether to believe it?
3. What do you do to check stock outside the app, if anything? Why?
````

````
# Interview Synthesis v1

Shelfwise: receiving a delivery and recording it in stock.

**Sources**
- P1: owner of a small grocery shop. In person, 30 min, 2026-10-20 (`interview-notes-p1.md`).
- P2: stock keeper at a stationery shop. Video call, 25 min, 2026-10-22 (`interview-notes-p2.md`).

**How to read this:** There are only two participants, so these are signals, not patterns. Text in quotation marks is quoted in the notes. Everything else is the interviewer's summary. Lines marked *Reading* are my interpretation, not something a participant said.

## Research question 1: In which step do mistakes or delays happen when receiving a delivery?

**Step 4 (record differences) is unclear**
- P1: "I never know if I should edit the order or the stock."

**The order of the steps changes when the shop is busy**
- P1 signed for the delivery before counting it: "The driver came at 8, I was alone at the till, so I signed the paper first and counted later."
- P2: deliveries often arrive during busy hours, and recording waits until the shop is quiet (summary, not a quote).

**Steps 2 and 3 happen in different places**
- P2 says counting and comparing happen in different places, "so I keep a paper in my pocket".

*Reading:* Both participants described delays between steps, and neither described a single slow step. The delays came from serving customers, not from the app. The only problem either of them placed in the app was at step 4.

## Research question 2: Where do differences come from, and how are they fixed once found?

**Where differences come from**
- P2: "The driver's note said 60 packs of pens, I counted 58." The supplier sent fewer than the note said.
- P1 signed before counting, so the delivery was accepted before anyone checked it.

**How they're fixed**
- P1: "When something is short I just change the number in the app. I don't write down why."
- P2: "I put the difference in the comment box but nobody reads it."
- P2 also wrote the correct count on the driver's note: "I wrote 58 on the driver note and he took it away."

*Reading:* Neither participant has a record of a difference that anyone else uses. P1 keeps no reason. P2's comments go unread, and the only written proof left with the driver. Neither participant mentioned an item that arrived without being ordered, which is the other case in the brief's scenario.

## Research question 3: What limits does counting at the shelf put on recording in the app?

**Counts are kept on paper and entered later**
- P1 counts at the shelf on a notepad and types the numbers in "at the end of the day, if I remember".
- P2 counts in the stockroom, then walks to the computer "maybe 20 minutes later, after serving customers".
- P2: "so I keep a paper in my pocket".

*Reading:* In both shops, counting and recording are separated by paper and by time, from 20 minutes to the end of the day. Both are chances to forget or lose a count. P2 counts in the stockroom, not at the shelf, so Known 6 ("counting usually happens at the shelf") isn't true everywhere.

## Research question 4: Why is recorded stock different from real stock, and how much do users trust it?

**Trust depends on the kind of item**
- P1: "I don't trust the stock number for pasta or rice. I go and look at the shelf."
- P2: "The numbers are fine for big items. Small items are always off by a few."

**Possible causes (taken from the answers to research questions 2 and 3)**
- Counts are entered late, or forgotten (P1: "if I remember").
- Stock is edited without a reason (P1).
- Differences are noted where nobody reads them (P2).

*Reading:* Both participants distrust small, numerous items and check the shelf instead of the app. Neither said directly why the numbers are wrong. The causes above are my inference.

## Did not fit a research question

- P1 used a spreadsheet before: "the spreadsheet was slower but I knew what I typed." This may be relevant to trust (research question 4). It is also the only comparison with another tool, which relates to research question 5.

## Gaps for the next round

- Two participants, one from each shop type. No owner and stock keeper from the same shop.
- P2 was interviewed by video call, so their counting wasn't observed. Research question 3 still needs someone in the shop to watch.
- Nobody described an item that arrived without being ordered.
- We haven't heard how the supplier settles differences, for example credit notes or re-delivery.
````

````
# Shelfwise interview synthesis v1

Sources: interview-notes-p1.md (P1, grocery owner, in person, 30 min, 2026-10-20) and interview-notes-p2.md (P2, stationery stock keeper, video call, 25 min, 2026-10-22).
Only 2 participants, so these are signals to check, not findings. Quotes are taken word for word from the notes.

## RQ1: In which step do mistakes or delays happen when receiving a delivery?
- **Deliveries arrive when staff are busy, so recording gets pushed back.** Both participants delay the work. P2 says recording waits until the shop is quiet. P1: "The driver came at 8, I was alone at the till, so I signed the paper first and counted later."
- **The order is confirmed before anything is counted.** P1 signed for the delivery before counting it (quote above).
- **Step 4 (record differences) is confusing.** P1: "I never know if I should edit the order or the stock."
- **Steps 2 and 3 happen in different places.** P2 counts in one place and compares at the computer, "so I keep a paper in my pocket".

## RQ2: Where do differences come from, and how are they fixed once found?
- **Short deliveries are one source.** P2: "The driver's note said 60 packs of pens, I counted 58."
- **Fixes are made outside the app, or without a reason.**
  - P2: "I wrote 58 on the driver note and he took it away." The only record left the shop.
  - P1: "When something is short I just change the number in the app. I don't write down why."
  - P2: "I put the difference in the comment box but nobody reads it."
- **Pattern:** neither participant leaves a record of why a difference happened that anyone can find later. This fits Known 7 (no reliable data on why differences happen).

## RQ3: What limits does counting at the shelf put on recording in the app?
- **Counts go onto paper first and are typed in later.**
  - P1 uses a notepad and types the numbers in "at the end of the day, if I remember".
  - P2 walks to the computer "maybe 20 minutes later, after serving customers".
- **The gap between counting and recording depends on memory and spare time.**
- **Counting doesn't always happen at the shelf.** P2 counts in the stockroom, which questions Known 6 ("counting usually happens at the shelf").

## RQ4: Why is recorded stock different from real stock, and how much do users trust it?
- **Trust depends on the item.**
  - P1: "I don't trust the stock number for pasta or rice. I go and look at the shelf."
  - P2: "The numbers are fine for big items. Small items are always off by a few."
- **Possible causes, from RQ1–3:** late or forgotten entry, edits with no reason recorded, and differences that are written on the driver's note or in an unread comment. These are links we've drawn, not something participants said.

## Outside the four questions
- **Earlier tool:** P1 used a spreadsheet before: "the spreadsheet was slower but I knew what I typed." This may be useful for RQ5 (other tools).

## Open questions for the next round
- Which items count as "small", and why are they off? (P2)
- Is signing for a delivery before counting it common?
- Who is meant to read the comment box?
````
