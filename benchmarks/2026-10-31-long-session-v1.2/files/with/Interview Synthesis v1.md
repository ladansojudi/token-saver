# Interview Synthesis v1 — Shelfwise receiving flow

Sources: interview-notes-p1.md (P1, grocery shop owner, in person) and interview-notes-p2.md (P2, stationery stock keeper, video call). Both files say they are made-up notes for a course exercise.
Only 2 participants, so treat everything below as early signals, not findings.

Key: text in "quotes" is quoted in the notes. Lines marked *(notes)* are the note-taker's paraphrase. Lines marked **Interpretation** are mine, not what participants said.

## RQ1 — In which step do mistakes or delays happen when receiving a delivery?
- P1: "The driver came at 8, I was alone at the till, so I signed the paper first and counted later."
- P1, about step 4 (record differences): "I never know if I should edit the order or the stock."
- P2, about steps 2 and 3 happening in different places: "so I keep a paper in my pocket".
- P2 *(notes)*: deliveries often arrive during busy hours, so recording waits until the shop is quiet.

**Interpretation:** Two possible weak points: step 4, where P1 doesn't know what to edit, and the gap between counting and recording, which both participants have. P1 confirms receipt before counting, so the five steps don't always happen in the listed order.

## RQ2 — Where do differences come from, and how are they fixed once found?
- P2: "The driver's note said 60 packs of pens, I counted 58. I wrote 58 on the driver note and he took it away."
- P1: "When something is short I just change the number in the app. I don't write down why."
- P2: "I put the difference in the comment box but nobody reads it."

**Interpretation:** The only source of a difference anyone described is a short delivery (P2). The notes don't say where other differences come from. Fixes leave little trace: P1 overwrites the stock number and doesn't record a reason, P2's comment goes unread, and P2's written count leaves with the driver.

## RQ3 — What limits does counting at the shelf put on recording in the app?
- P1 *(notes)*: counts at the shelf on a notepad, types the numbers in "at the end of the day, if I remember".
- P2 *(notes)*: counts in the stockroom, then walks to the computer "maybe 20 minutes later, after serving customers".
- P2: "so I keep a paper in my pocket".

**Interpretation:** Both participants carry their counts on paper and type them in later. The gap runs from about 20 minutes (P2) to the end of the day (P1), and depends on memory and how busy the shop is. Known 6 ("counting usually happens at the shelf") holds for P1 but not for P2, who counts in the stockroom.

## RQ4 — Why is recorded stock different from real stock, and how much do users trust it?
- P1: "I don't trust the stock number for pasta or rice. I go and look at the shelf."
- P2: "The numbers are fine for big items. Small items are always off by a few."
- P1, comparing the app with the spreadsheet they used before: "the spreadsheet was slower but I knew what I typed."

**Interpretation:** Trust seems to depend on the type of item. It's lower for items like pasta and rice (P1) and for small items (P2), and both participants check the shelf or expect errors for those. Neither participant explained why the numbers are off. Late or forgotten entries and unexplained edits (RQ2, RQ3) might be a cause, but that's my guess and not something they said.

## Still open
- Where differences come from apart from short deliveries.
- Whether other participants also confirm receipt before counting.
- Why specific item types are off. A follow-up question about one item could help.
