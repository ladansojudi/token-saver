## RQ1 — In which step do mistakes or delays happen when receiving a delivery?
**Status:** Partly
**External research:** desk-research.md: "Not found (these need real users)". Related, linked by me: RQ6 found errors more common for items with frequent deliveries, and receiving errors named as a common cause.
**User evidence:** P1 finds step 4 confusing: "I never know if I should edit the order or the stock." P1 signs for the delivery before counting. Both P1 and P2 record late because they're busy (*notes*).
**Still unknown:** whether step 4 confuses others as well; how often steps happen out of order; where actual mistakes occur, as opposed to delays.

## RQ2 — Where do differences come from, and how are they fixed once found?
**Status:** Partly
**External research:** desk-research.md: "Not found". Related, linked by me: RQ6 trade article names shrinkage and receiving errors as common causes.
**User evidence:** Short delivery: P2: "The driver's note said 60 packs of pens, I counted 58…" Fixes: P1 overwrites the number, "I don't write down why"; P2 uses the comment box, "nobody reads it".
**Still unknown:** sources of differences other than short deliveries (for example items that were never ordered, as in the brief's scenario, which isn't evidence); whether differences are ever followed up with the supplier.

## RQ3 — What limits does counting at the shelf put on recording in the app?
**Status:** Partly
**External research:** desk-research.md: "Not found".
**User evidence:** Both carry counts on paper and type them in later. P1 at the end of the day, "if I remember"; P2 "maybe 20 minutes later". P2 counts in the stockroom, not at the shelf, which goes against Known 6.
**Still unknown:** whether counts get lost or changed on the way to the computer (nobody described a case); how common counting away from the shelf is.

## RQ4 — Why is recorded stock different from real stock, and how much do users trust it?
**Status:** Partly. The trust part has some evidence; the why part is open.
**External research:** desk-research.md: "Not found". Related, linked by me: RQ6 study found inaccurate records for about 65% of audited items.
**User evidence:** P1: "I don't trust the stock number for pasta or rice." P2: "Small items are always off by a few." P1 about their old spreadsheet: "I knew what I typed."
**Still unknown:** why the numbers are off. Neither participant said. A link to late or unexplained entries is my guess, not evidence.

## RQ5 — How do other stock apps handle receiving deliveries?
**Status:** Open
**External research:** desk-research.md: "Not found".
**User evidence:** None about other stock apps. P1 used a spreadsheet before, which is a different kind of tool.
**Still unknown:** the whole question. It needs a review of other tools.

## RQ6 — What does published research say about stock accuracy and delivery differences?
**Status:** Answered, with limits
**External research:** Inventory records inaccurate for about 65% of audited items; errors more common for items with frequent deliveries (https://example.org/study-inventory-accuracy). Shrinkage and receiving errors are common causes in small shops (https://example.org/trade-receiving-errors).
**User evidence:** Not needed for this question.
**Still unknown:** whether the 65% study covered small shops; the small-shop claim comes from a trade article, not a study. The links are placeholder sources.
