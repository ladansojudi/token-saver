# Interview Synthesis v1

Sources: interview-notes-p1.md (P1, grocery shop owner, in person, 2026-10-20) and interview-notes-p2.md (P2, stationery shop stock keeper, video call, 2026-10-22).

Text in quotation marks is a participant's own words, as written in the notes. Text without quotation marks is the note-taker's paraphrase. Our interpretations are kept separate, under "Our interpretation". With only two participants, these are early signals, not patterns.

## Research question 1: Where mistakes or delays happen when receiving a delivery

**What participants said**
- P1 signed for the delivery before counting: "The driver came at 8, I was alone at the till, so I signed the paper first and counted later."
- P1 finds step 4 (record differences) confusing: "I never know if I should edit the order or the stock."
- P2 does step 2 (count) and step 3 (compare) in different places, "so I keep a paper in my pocket".
- P2: deliveries often arrive during busy hours, and recording waits until the shop is quiet. (paraphrase)

**Our interpretation**
- Delays seem to come from timing (deliveries arrive when staff are busy or alone), not from the app itself.
- Step 4 is where P1 is unsure what to do in the app.
- Neither participant follows the five steps in the brief's order or in one place, which challenges Known 3.

## Research question 2: Where differences come from and how they are fixed

**What participants said**
- P2 found a short delivery: "The driver's note said 60 packs of pens, I counted 58. I wrote 58 on the driver note and he took it away."
- P1: "When something is short I just change the number in the app. I don't write down why."
- P2: "I put the difference in the comment box but nobody reads it."

**Our interpretation**
- Differences do get found, but the record of them gets lost: it leaves with the driver (P2), never gets written down (P1), or sits in a comment box nobody reads (P2).
- Both participants fix differences in different ways, and neither way leaves a reason anyone can look up later. This may explain Known 7 (there's no reliable data on why differences happen).

## Research question 3: How counting away from the computer limits recording in the app

**What participants said**
- P1 counts at the shelf on a notepad and types the numbers in "at the end of the day, if I remember".
- P2 counts in the stockroom, then walks to the computer "maybe 20 minutes later, after serving customers".
- P2 keeps the count on "a paper in my pocket".

**Our interpretation**
- Both participants count away from the computer and use paper to carry the numbers over. The gap between counting and recording runs from about 20 minutes (P2) to the end of the day (P1), and P1's "if I remember" suggests counts can get lost along the way.
- Known 6 is only partly right: P1 counts at the shelf, but P2 counts in the stockroom. This research question may need rewording to "counting away from the computer".

## Research question 4: Why recorded stock differs from real stock, and how much users trust it

**What participants said**
- P1: "I don't trust the stock number for pasta or rice. I go and look at the shelf."
- P2: "The numbers are fine for big items. Small items are always off by a few."

**Our interpretation**
- Trust depends on the type of item, not the app as a whole. Both participants distrust small or high-volume items.
- Possible causes come from the earlier questions: late or forgotten entries (RQ3), and differences fixed without a record (RQ2). Neither participant said this directly.

## Doesn't fit a research question

- P1 used a spreadsheet before: "the spreadsheet was slower but I knew what I typed." This could be useful for RQ5 (other tools) and for trust (RQ4).
